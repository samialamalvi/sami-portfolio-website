About

I solved my first Rubik's cube at eleven, and I've been looking for structure in complicated things ever since. That instinct carried me through math and physics in Dhaka — where I scored the world's highest marks across five International A-Level units — to computer science at FIU, where I now research how neural networks perform when you strip them down to the fewest connections that still work.

Outside research, most of my time goes to teaching. I hold weekly office hours for Discrete Structures, tutor six different CS courses over the summer, and serve as a resident assistant for 500+ students. I like the part of both jobs where something confusing becomes obvious.
